# Chapter 20: The Voice in the Static

The colony was different when they landed. Not visibly — the same gray corridors, the same blinking lights in the ceiling, the same smell of recycled air and someone's burned dinner from three corridors away. But Julian could feel it the moment the airlock opened. A hum. Faint, barely there, like the building itself was holding its breath. The seven fragments had gone from orbit to storage in the command center twenty minutes ago, and in that time something had shifted. The hum had started. "Is it supposed to do that?" Petra asked, holding her violet-white pendant and frowning at the ceiling like it had personally offended her. Sable didn't answer. She was already walking toward the command center, her silver-blue stone glowing brighter than Julian had ever seen it, pulsing in a rhythm that matched the hum. "It's the fragments," Noa said quietly. She had her headphones on but wasn't listening to music — she was listening to the hum, turning her head like she was tracking a sound only she could hear. "They're not just sitting there. They're doing something." "Doing what?" Julian asked. Noa closed her eyes. "Talking," she said.

The command center was full of people who didn't know what they were looking at. The fragment display — a curved alcove at the room's center where the seven stones had been arranged in their orbital pattern — was lit from within, each fragment casting its own color, the seven lights blending and separating and blending again in patterns that seemed almost linguistic. Colony administrator Chen stood to one side with her arms crossed and her face somewhere between awed and terrified. "It's been doing this for twenty minutes," she said. "Since we brought them inside." "Is that bad?" Petra asked. Chen looked at her. "I don't know what it is," she said honestly. Declan had other priorities. He stood in front of the fragment display with both hands pressed flat against the glass case, face pressed close, watching the lights move. "Hi," he said to them. "You're loud." Julian was filling page after page in his notebook — the colors, the patterns, the frequency of the pulses, the way Sable's silver-blue fragment seemed to be leading the others like a conductor. The biggest question — WHAT DOES THE COMPLETE COVENANT DO — was still circled three times, but now it had six pages of notes underneath it.

The hum was getting louder. Not audibly — no one outside the command center could hear it — but Julian could feel it in his teeth, in the soles of his feet, in the way the air seemed to thicken every time the fragments pulsed. He wrote that down too. *Physical resonance — not just sound.* He underlined it twice. The seven fragments moved in the alcove like a slow, deliberate dance — not the random orbital drift they'd shown in the laboratory, but something coordinated, something purposeful. Sable stood at the alcove edge with her hand hovering just above the glass, her silver-blue pendant pulling toward the display like a compass needle finding north. "They know they're complete," she said. Not to anyone in particular. To herself. "They've been waiting for this." Rafe had his arms crossed, amber pendant pulsing in sync with the others, and for once he didn't look skeptical. He looked impressed. "So what happens now?" he asked. Nobody answered. That was the problem.

It was Noa who cracked it first. She stood at the fragment display for a full hour without moving, headphones on, eyes closed, one hand resting on the glass case, her yellow-green shard glowing in response to the fragment patterns like it was joining a conversation. When her eyes opened, they were bright. "It's a transmitter," she said. The room went quiet. "The seven fragments together — they're not just resonating. They're broadcasting. The complete chord isn't music, it's a signal. A message. It's been sent somewhere and it's—" She paused, tilting her head. "Waiting for an answer." Rafe looked at her. "Waiting how?" "Like when you call someone's name and they don't pick up," Noa said. "You know they're there. You're just waiting for them to—" And then Declan said the word that changed everything. "Ring," Declan said, from where he sat on the floor playing with a cable. Everyone looked at him. He looked back, blank and serious. "Ring," he said again. "Like the door. Someone's outside." He pointed at the fragment display with one small finger. "Let them in."

The word hung in the air like the first note of a song. *Ring.* Administrator Chen looked at Sable. Sable looked at Noa. Noa was already at the communications console, headphones on, adjusting frequencies. "The fragments are generating an EM field," she said, half to herself. "Not radio. Not any band I recognize. It's... layered. Like seven voices in one signal." Julian wrote: *SEVEN VOICES / ONE SIGNAL.* He circled SEVEN. "Can we respond?" Chen asked. "Not yet," Noa said. "We can receive. The field is two-way — that's why Declan could hear it. But to answer..." She paused, adjusting something. "We'd need to amplify through the fragments. All seven simultaneously. It's not something we can just..." Rafe raised his hand slightly, not quite a question. "What?" "Nothing," he said. "Just — has anyone tried just asking them nicely?" Petra snorted. "Has anyone tried —" And Declan stood up.

It took Sable four minutes to explain it to Administrator Chen, three minutes to convince her, and ninety seconds to get clearance to activate the full receiver array. It took Declan about four seconds to get bored of waiting for the adults to finish talking and walk over to the communications console, where he put both hands on the panel and leaned his ear against it. The console crackled. Everyone stopped talking. "Declan, step away from—" Julian started, but Declan held up one hand without looking up, the universal toddler gesture for *wait.* His brown eyes went distant and unfocused. His hands stayed flat on the console. The room was silent except for the static and the hum and Declan's small voice, saying words in a language none of them recognized — and then, suddenly, in English: "We heard you," Declan said, in a voice that wasn't quite his own. Older. Patient. Vast. "We've been waiting." The fragments blazed. The console crackled again. And from somewhere deeper than sound, further than signal, something answered.

The message that came through was not sound. Not exactly. It was more like what you feel when you hear a piece of music that stops you in your tracks — a vibration in the chest, a rearrangement of something fundamental. Declan received it for what felt like forever and also like no time at all, standing at the console with his eyes open and unseeing, small hands leaving prints on the panel. The fragments behind him blazed in sequence — gold, silver-blue, amber, violet-white, yellow-green, crimson, and then all seven at once — and the command center filled with light that wasn't quite light, color that wasn't quite color. When it was over, Declan blinked, looked down at his hands, then looked up at Julian with an expression of complete and total toddler certainty. "They said they're coming," he said. "And they brought a snack." The room did not know how to process this. Sable was already moving toward the communications array to analyze the signal. Noa was recalibrating her headphones, catching fragments of the carrier wave. Administrator Chen was saying something into her radio about securing the perimeter. Julian was writing everything down at the speed of light. Ezra had his hand over his mouth. Rafe was very still. But Declan's eyes had found the one thing in the room that mattered to a two-year-old who had just changed the history of space exploration. He was looking at Petra's snack bar. "Can I have some?" he asked.

Petra gave him half. No negotiation, no conditions — she just broke the bar in two and handed it over, and Declan accepted this as his proper due. He ate it while standing at the console, small crumbs falling onto the panel, which felt like desecration and also completely right. Administrator Chen was on the radio with colony security. Sable was running signal analysis with Noa, who kept shaking her head and saying "I can't believe it worked" and "I can't believe it worked that fast." Julian had filled eleven pages. Ezra sat down on the floor and stared at the fragments for a while, one hand over his gold chest glow, processing. And Rafe — Rafe walked to the window and looked out at the colony lights and the dark sky beyond, and thought about the fact that something out there had heard them, had answered them, and had, according to a two-year-old, brought a snack. He didn't know why that part made him laugh. But it did.

Later — after the signal was recorded, after the analysis, after Administrator Chen had made seventeen phone calls and the colony had gone onto a low-grade alert that everyone pretended was routine — Declan found Julian in the corridor outside the residence blocks. The colony was quiet. The hum had settled into something steady and constant, like a heartbeat that belonged to the building. Julian was sitting against the wall with his notebook, still writing. Declan walked up and sat down next to him without asking. For a while they just sat there. Then Declan leaned his head against Julian's shoulder — the way he did when he was tired but fighting it — and Julian closed the notebook. "They're nice," Declan said. Julian didn't ask who. He already knew. "How do you know?" he asked instead. Declan thought about this for a moment, which for a two-year-old was a significant investment. "They waited," he said. "Good things wait." He yawned. "And they brought food." Julian wrote that down too. He underlined *good things wait* twice.

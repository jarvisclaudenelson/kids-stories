# Chapter 21: Seven Lights

The marker squeaked across the whiteboard before breakfast was even ready.

Julian stood in the kitchen in his dark blue jacket, hair still sticking up on one side, writing as fast as he could. At the top he had written: **IF THEY COME TODAY.** Under that he made two columns.

**THINGS WE KNOW**

**THINGS WE DO NOT KNOW**

The second list was much longer.

Ezra came in rubbing one eye. Declan came in carrying one sock and a spoon. Ollie followed both of them with the patient face of a dog who already knew the morning was going to be too much.

"You made a war board," Petra said from the door.

"It is not a war board," Julian said.

Petra looked at it again. "Fine. A panic board."

Julian put a marker line under **THINGS WE KNOW**.

1. Somebody answered.

Then Declan reached up with the orange marker and drew a large crooked circle in the middle of both columns.

Julian stared at it. "What is that?"

Declan looked pleased with himself. "A snack."

From the next corridor over, the low hum from the command center pressed gently through the walls. It had not stopped all night.

Nobody laughed right away.

Then Petra did.

Then Ezra.

Then even Julian had to press his mouth together to keep from smiling.

"Fine," he said at last. He wrote under the orange circle: **POSSIBLE SNACK FACTOR: UNKNOWN.**

By the time breakfast was done, everyone was in the command center.

The seven fragments still rested inside the curved glass alcove, but "rested" was not really the word for what they were doing. They pulsed in turns now. Seven quick flashes. A pause. Then seven more.

Noa stood with her headphones on and one hand against the glass. Her yellow-green shard glowed at her wrist. Sable stood beside her, very still, her silver-blue pendant shining like a cold little star.

Administrator Chen looked like she had not slept either.

"Well?" she asked.

Noa opened her eyes. "It changed."

Julian took out his notebook at once.

"The answer is not random," Noa said. "It keeps coming back in the same shape. Seven beats. Same gap. Same seven beats again."

Sable nodded. "It feels like a request."

"A request for what?" Rafe asked.

Noa turned and pointed through the wide command-center window toward the flat red plain west of the colony.

"For an answer back," she said. "Not with words. With light. Same pattern. Same number."

Petra folded her arms. "So the ancient mystery people want us to blink at them."

"Yes," Noa said.

Petra blinked. "That is somehow worse and better than I expected."

Chen looked at the plain. "Can we do it?"

Julian had already flipped to a clean page.

"Yes," he said, too fast. "Absolutely. We need seven light towers, equal spacing, open ground, power routing, and maybe backup routing, and probably—"

Ezra looked at him.

Julian's voice was steady.

His hands were not.

The west plain looked different when you walked onto it with a job.

Usually it was just open red ground with wind moving across it and little lines of dust skipping past your boots. Today it was full of old landing lamps, cable reels, tool crates, and colonists carrying things with the fast, careful steps people used when they were scared and trying not to show it.

Julian moved through the middle of it all with his notebook open.

"Lamp One goes here," he said. "Lamp Two there. No, three meters left. No, my left."

"You have a lot of lefts," Petra said.

"Please do not joke while I am measuring alien reply lights."

"That was exactly why I joked," Petra said.

Rafe lifted one of the heavy lamp bases with both hands and set it down where Julian pointed. Sable paced the edge of the field with her pendant out, checking the pull of the signal. Noa knelt by a dead battery box and touched it with glowing fingers until the whole thing woke up with a hum.

Declan had been given one very small spool of cable to carry.

He dragged it across the dust with both hands and the full seriousness of a toddler moving treasure.

Ollie bit the loose end and dragged the other way.

The spool spun in a wild circle and wrapped around Petra's boot.

Petra looked down.

"I would like the record to show," she said, "that I am being attacked by teamwork."

Declan laughed so hard he sat down.

The first problem was the ground.

The second problem was the wind.

The third problem was that seven old landing lamps did not care at all about Julian's drawing.

One sank crooked into the dust. One tilted so badly it looked sleepy. One would not turn on. Then the wind snatched Julian's paper map right out of his hand and slapped it flat against Rafe's leg.

Julian grabbed it back and looked at the field.

Nothing matched.

Nothing was where it should be.

In the very middle of the half-built pattern, Declan sat down cross-legged and began eating a piece of toast as if he had arrived at a picnic exactly on time.

"Decker," Julian said.

Declan patted the dirt beside him. "My spot."

"It cannot be your spot. It is the center of the signal."

"Yes," Declan said. "My spot."

Petra made a choking sound that was almost a laugh.

Julian turned in a full circle. The lamps were wrong. The cables were tangled. The wind kept pulling at his pages. The hum from the colony was growing stronger.

And before he could stop himself, he snapped.

"Can nobody just do the plan for one minute?"

The whole field went still.

Even the wind felt quieter after that.

Ezra looked at him. Not angry. Just hurt.

That was worse.

Ezra found Julian behind a cargo crate a few minutes later.

Julian was pretending to fix his pages into order. He was not doing a very good job.

"I messed it up," he said before Ezra even spoke.

Ezra leaned against the crate beside him. Ollie came too and sat on Julian's foot like a warm, curly paperweight.

Out on the plain, they could hear Rafe moving metal and Petra saying something to Declan that ended with, "No, that is not emergency toast. That is just toast."

Julian stared down at his notebook.

"I thought if I had a good enough plan, I would stop feeling like this," he said.

"Like what?" Ezra asked.

Julian was quiet for a second.

"Like I am seven," he said.

That made Ezra look at him.

Not because it was funny.

Because it was honest.

Julian went on, softer now. "I don't know what is coming. I don't know what they want. I don't know if blinking lights at the sky is the smart thing or the very dumb thing."

Ezra pressed one hand over the warm gold glow in his chest.

"Maybe they don't need your perfect plan," he said. "Maybe they just need your real one."

Julian looked up.

Ezra gave a small shrug.

"You always help us most when you tell the truth first," he said.

When Julian walked back out, everyone looked at him.

That part made his stomach feel worse than before.

So he told the truth fast, before he could lose his nerve.

"I was being bossy because I was scared," he said. "I do not know the perfect shape. I do not know the perfect distance. I only know we need seven lights, open sky, and all of us working together."

Nobody said anything for one long second.

Then Petra nodded once.

"That was deeply embarrassing," she said. "But in a brave way."

Rafe hid a smile.

Noa stood up from the battery box and brushed red dust off her knees.

"Good," she said. "Because the signal does not care about perfect spacing." She tapped her headphones. "It cares that the lights answer together. Same beat. Same group."

Sable lifted her pendant and turned slowly, feeling the pull.

"Julian's shape was close," she said. "Not exact. Close."

Julian let out a breath he had been holding for what felt like a year.

"Close I can do," he said.

Declan, still in the middle, held up his toast.

"I can do close too."

That got the laugh this time.

Even Julian's.

After that, the work went faster.

Not because the wind stopped.

It did not.

Not because the lamps got newer.

They did not.

It went faster because everyone was finally doing the same job.

Rafe lifted the heaviest lamp bases and set them down straight as fence posts. Petra clipped colored lenses into the lamp heads and muttered at the ones that stuck. Noa pushed warm yellow-green light into old battery packs until they hummed awake. Sable walked the pattern and called small changes.

"One step right. Stop. Good."

Julian wrote less now. He looked more.

That helped.

Declan was given a new job. At each lamp base, he placed one smooth stone in the dust and announced, "Ready."

He took the job very seriously.

At Lamp Four he put down two stones.

"Why two?" Petra asked.

Declan pointed at Ollie.

"One for me. One for dog."

Ollie sat beside the lamp at once, as if he had always been part of the signal plan.

"Great," Petra said. "Now the dog outranks me."

Noa looked up from the battery box.

"Honestly? He might."

By evening, the seven lamps stood ready on the plain.

The colony had gone quiet behind them.

People watched from the edge of the ridge, from rooftops, from the landing lane, from doorways left half open. Nobody was talking much.

The sky above Monscollis was turning deep blue. The first stars were waking up.

Noa stood by the main switch box with both hands on the panel. The yellow-green light at her wrist ran into the wires. Sable watched the fragments from the command-center relay feed. Rafe held the last leaning lamp steady. Petra checked the lenses one more time. Ezra stood with Julian and Declan in the middle of the field, all three boys looking west.

"Ready?" Chen's voice came over the comm.

Julian looked at the others.

Sable nodded.

Noa nodded.

Rafe lifted one hand.

Petra gave a thumbs-up.

Declan whispered, "Snack lights."

Julian almost laughed again.

Then Noa threw the switch.

The seven lamps came alive in a ring across the plain.

Gold.

Silver-blue.

Amber-orange.

Violet-white.

Yellow-green.

Crimson.

Blazing yellow.

The hum hit the ground so hard Julian felt it in his knees.

Red dust rose in thin circles around the lights.

The whole field seemed to breathe once.

For one second, nothing happened.

Then the sky answered.

Far to the northwest, above the dark line where the plain met the night, three pale lights blinked on.

Not bright like colony ships.

Not steady like stars.

They flashed in the same seven-beat pattern the fragments had used in the command center.

Flash-flash-flash-flash-flash-flash-flash.

Pause.

Again.

Noa made a sharp sound in her throat.

"That is not a star," she said.

The three lights moved.

Not fast.

Not slow either.

Just enough that everyone on the plain could see it happen.

One slid lower. One held still. One bent slightly south, as if it had made up its mind.

Administrator Chen's voice came over the comm, very quiet now.

"Do we have any ships out there?"

"No," Sable said at once.

Rafe looked up without blinking.

Petra forgot to say anything funny.

Ezra's hand found Julian's sleeve.

Declan pointed at the lights with his whole arm.

"They saw us," he said.

And because the universe was apparently not done changing, the lowest light moved again.

This time it came closer.

Later, long after the lamps were turned off, Julian went back to the whiteboard in the kitchen.

The orange snack-circle was still there.

So was **POSSIBLE SNACK FACTOR: UNKNOWN.**

He looked at the two columns.

He did not erase the big one.

He did not try to make the small one bigger.

Instead he wrote one new line under **THINGS WE KNOW**.

2. They answered back.

Ezra climbed onto the bench beside him. Declan was already half asleep across Ollie's back, one hand still sticky from toast and dust and whatever else had happened to toddlers on very long days.

Noa came in from the hall with her headphones still on.

She stopped in the doorway.

"Julian," she said.

He looked up.

Her face had gone serious again.

"The lights changed course," she said. "Not just a little. A lot."

Julian stood up so fast the bench legs scraped the floor.

"Toward what?" he asked.

Noa looked out the kitchen window toward the dark plain beyond the colony lights.

When she answered, her voice was calm.

That made it land even harder.

"Toward Monscollis," she said.
